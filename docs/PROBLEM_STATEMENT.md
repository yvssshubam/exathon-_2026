# Problem statement

**Arena:** Track 01, Money, Risk & Regulation.

> A bank nodal officer can't act on a cyber-fraud complaint within the golden hour, while the money is still in the
> first account, because night-time complaints wait for the desk to open and every hop is then traced and held by
> hand. If this worked, the victim's money would be held before it is withdrawn, and only the victim's share of it,
> so honest customers further down the trail keep their accounts.

**Who the nodal officer is:** the person at each bank who receives cyber-fraud complaints from the police through the
national reporting system and must find the payment, hold what is left, and report where the rest went.

## Why it matters

- Police advise reporting within the first hour, because that is when the money is still in the first one or two
  accounts. ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/theweek/current/2026/08/14/how-gaps-in-banking-and-policing-are-fuelling-cyber-fraud.html))
- Only about 29% of reported money was held nationally in H1 2026. ([Anantam IAS](https://anantamias.com/?p=36896))
- In Rajasthan, a complaint made at night missed the hold because bank representatives work banking hours.
  ([The420](https://the420.in/?p=94888))
- Of ₹7,647 crore put on hold from April 2021 to November 2025, only ₹167 crore (2.18%) had been returned to victims.
  ([MHA SOP, 2 Jan 2026, para 2](https://the420.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/sop.pdf))
- Over-freezing is the rational choice today: the notice threatens action for not complying, with no consequence for
  holding too much. One High Court settled 105 petitions against account freezes in a single judgment.
  ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/08/223-freeze-amount-rajasthan-hcs-cybercrime/))

Build zone: the Layer-1 bank, inside the golden hour. Impact zone: fewer honest receivers frozen, so fewer grievances
later.
